# Find and qualify IT roles in France

[Back to the guide](README.md)

23 November 2026 · A practical guide to discovery, screening, networking and follow-up for candidates who want to work in France.

Start with two role families you can support with concrete examples. Search each under two common titles. Run a France-wide remote search and a separate search around Paris-based employers. Track the location, attendance and working-language requirements for each role. This keeps an interesting employer visible while you check whether the actual work arrangement fits.

## Where to look

Apec surveyed 1,150 private businesses with at least 10 employees that had hired at least one person in a managerial or professional role during 2025. Of those businesses, 84% had used a vacancy ad and 54% named ads as the channel that most often yielded the candidate they ultimately hired. The survey covers recruitment across occupations in that employment category. For an IT search, start with posted roles and use conversations to discover adjacent teams, verify the work and make well-targeted introductions. [Apec, 2026 recruitment practices for managerial and professional staff](https://corporate.apec.fr/home/nos-etudes/toutes-nos-etudes/pratiques-de-recrutement-de-cadres-2026.html)

## Search by the work, then by title

Choose from the vocabulary below according to what you want to own. Add one responsibility term when a title is too broad, then remove it periodically to catch sparse postings and variant titles.

| Work you want to do | Job titles to try | Extra terms to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production reliability | `SRE`, `Site Reliability Engineer`, `Production Engineer`, `IT Operations Engineer` | `incident management`, `on-call`, `observability`, `SLO` |
| Cloud and developer platforms | `Platform Engineer`, `Cloud Engineer`, `DevOps Engineer`, `Developer Experience Engineer` | `Kubernetes`, `Terraform`, `GitOps`, `developer experience`, `developer platform`, `self-service` |
| Software and quality | `Software Engineer`, `Backend Engineer`, `QA Engineer`, `SDET`, `Test Automation Engineer` | `API`, `CI/CD`, `integration tests`, `production deployment` |
| IT systems and support | `Systems Administrator`, `Systems Engineer`, `Application Support Engineer`, `Level 2 Support Technician`, `Level 3 Support Technician` | `ITSM`, `Linux`, `troubleshooting`, `monitoring` |
| Security and resilience | `DevSecOps Engineer`, `Cloud Security Engineer`, `SOC Analyst`, `Incident Response Engineer` | `IAM`, `incident management`, `resilience`, `business continuity` |
| Data and AI production | `Data Engineer`, `ML Platform Engineer`, `MLOps Engineer`, `AI Infrastructure Engineer`, `Applied AI Engineer` | `model serving`, `inference`, `evaluation`, `monitoring`, `data governance` |
| Technical direction and management | `Tech Lead`, `Lead SRE`, `Engineering Manager`, `Head of Platform`, `IT Operations Manager` | `mentoring`, `budget`, `delivery`, `people management` |

Read the responsibilities to distinguish a platform built for internal developers, cloud delivery for clients, and production operations centred on incidents and on-call. An engineer who works with GitOps can find relevant work across those titles. A management search can also run in parallel with a technical-lead search if both are supported by evidence.

Try these on a board with location and workplace filters.

- Keywords `"site reliability engineer" OR "production engineer"`, location **France**, workplace **remote**. Run separate alerts where Boolean operators are unsupported.
- Keywords `"platform engineer" OR "cloud engineer"`, location **Paris or Île-de-France**. Review remote results first and qualify any promising hybrid exception.
- Keywords `"MLOps" OR "ML platform" OR "AI infrastructure"`, location **France**. Look for ownership of serving, model deployment, cost, reliability or evaluation before treating a title as a useful lead.
- If you need an English-speaking team, try `"backend engineer" AND "English-speaking"` with **France** as the location. Also save a title-only search so you see roles where the employer leaves the working language implicit.
- On a general search engine, try `site:jobs.lever.co "site reliability" "France"`, `site:job-boards.greenhouse.io "platform engineer" "Paris"` or `site:<company domain> careers "Kubernetes"`. Replace the example terms and follow through to the official posting. Search indexes can miss new roles and retain old pages.

Build the employer list in parallel. Pick a few teams from each type of organization you would realistically join. Try product and SaaS companies, regulated or infrastructure-heavy internal IT teams, public-sector digital teams and IT services firms. An incident write-up, a platform migration post or a conference talk can tell you which team does the work. Treat that information as a reason to research or contact the team. Use an active requisition or direct employer confirmation when you need to know whether it is hiring.

## Check the team's working language

An English job ad is a useful lead. Read the language requirements in the full posting and confirm what the team uses. An English-language recruiter screen can be followed by technical interviews or day-to-day work in French.

- **Application and interviews.** Ask whether the team accepts an English CV and which language it uses at each stage, including any exercise or presentation.
- **Day-to-day engineering.** Ask what language people use in stand-ups, written reviews and documentation.
- **Customers and on-call.** Ask whether you would handle customer calls, incident updates or escalations in French, and what level the team expects from day one. If the ad lists French as preferred, ask where it comes up and describe the level you can actually work at.

> Hi, I’m interested in the [role] on your team. Is an English CV suitable, and can the interviews be conducted in English? What language does the team use for day-to-day work, customer communication and on-call?

## Verify remote eligibility and office cadence

| What the ad says | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| `Remote from France`, `fully remote within France` | France residence permitted, employing entity or hiring arrangement, work hours or time-zone overlap, onboarding and any scheduled office days. |
| `EMEA remote`, `Europe remote`, `remote-first` | Employer's eligible countries for this opening, whether a salaried contract is available from France, and any required travel to a team hub. |
| `Remote work available`, `partly remote`, `hybrid` | Exact office or client location, minimum in-person days over a stated period, fixed days, team practices, and rules during onboarding or probation. |
| `Paris`, `Île-de-France`, `La Défense` | Whether this describes a headquarters, a hiring geography, a team office or a client site. Ask for the exact city or site before assessing a recurring commute. |
| `Occasional business travel`, `travel required`, `on-call` | For travel, ask how often, where and for how long. For on-call, ask about rotation, response expectations, compensation and whether interventions ever require attendance. |

The French government explains that remote-work arrangements can be set under a collective agreement, company charter or agreement between employee and employer. For each active lead, record the person or written document that confirmed the arrangement and the date. Reconfirm in writing before relying on it in an offer. With an IT services firm, ask whether the client site or client policy changes the actual office schedule for the assignment. [French government guidance on private-sector remote work](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F13851?lang=en)

> Hi, I’m interested in the role, especially [responsibility]. Can you confirm whether I can work from France, which office the team uses, and how often I’d be expected there? Is there a different schedule during onboarding or any additional business travel?

Keep promising roles with incomplete information marked **to verify**. Assess hybrid or on-site roles individually when the work is a strong fit and the location and required cadence are clear. A Paris employer can hire remotely, and an occasional offsite has different implications from weekly office days.

### If you are applying from abroad

Tell the recruiter where you are based now, where you would work after hiring, and whether you need employer support with work authorisation for France. If you plan to move, ask when the employer needs you in France and whether it supports the move. If you intend to stay in your current country, ask whether the company can employ you there for this role. For work authorisation, check the official requirements for your situation before relying on a start date. [France-Visas, salaried employment](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/activite-salariee) · [EURES, living and working in France](https://eures.europa.eu/living-and-working/living-and-working-conditions-europe/living-and-working-conditions-france_en)

> I’m currently based in [city and country] and would plan to work from [location]. Is that arrangement possible for this role? I would need employer support with French work authorisation. Is that available for this opening?

Include the question about work authorisation if you need employer support.

## Turn a lead into a conversation

- Start with former colleagues, collaborators, peers and relevant technical communities. Bring a specific question about an employer or its work. Ask for a referral if the person is comfortable referring you and has enough context to do so.
- Look through the CNCF events calendar and local groups, France Travail’s recruitment events and Apec workshops. An organizer or speaker can point you toward a relevant team. Look for event topics that match your work and check the organizer's current schedule before making plans. [CNCF events](https://www.cncf.io/events/) · [Cloud Native Paris](https://ocgroups.dev/cncf/group/24tk85c) · [France Travail events](https://www.francetravail.fr/candidat/vos-services-en-ligne/mes-evenements-emploi--un-outil.html) · [Apec centres](https://centre.apec.fr/fr)
- For a spontaneous application, point to a public team problem or project, give one credible example of related work and ask a small question. Take the formal careers route when an open requisition requests it. If the employer gave a timeline, follow it. For direct outreach without a timeline, one follow-up after about a week is a reasonable starting point. [France Travail, spontaneous application steps](https://labonneboite.francetravail.fr/questions-frequentes/les-etapes-de-la-candidature-spontanee)

Adapt this note with details about the team and your work.

> Hi [first name], I saw your team’s write-up on [specific topic]. I worked on [related result I can explain], and I’m looking for a role where I could contribute to [responsibility]. Is your team responsible for that work? If you have ten minutes, I’d love to hear how you handle [specific question]. Thanks, [your name].

For unexpected recruiter contact, start with the [recruiter and assignment verification steps](fieldwork.md#verify-the-recruiter-and-the-assignment).

## Run a measurable search

A starting week could include two short sessions to triage alerts and verify listings, a focused block for three to five eligible applications, and a block for two relevant conversations or introductions. Adjust the pace around interviews, energy and the number of eligible roles. A brief Friday review makes it easier to see which search terms, employers and introductions produced opportunities.

Use a private tracker with one row per requisition or exploratory employer conversation. Keep recruiting data outside public portfolios and community repositories.

| Fields | What they tell you |
| --- | --- |
| Company, team, title, requisition ID, canonical link, source and date seen | Where the opportunity came from, when it appeared and whether it is a duplicate. |
| Required responsibilities, evidence you can show, and open questions | Whether you can make a credible case and what you need to clarify. |
| Eligible from France, contract, office location, attendance, travel, policy source and date | Whether the arrangement fits. Carry unknowns through as questions. |
| Working language, interview language, and any French needed for customers or on-call | Which parts of the hiring process and job fit your language skills. |
| Date applied, approach, CV version, stage, next action and due date | Where effort is going and where the application needs attention. |
| Human replies, screens, technical stages and offers | Where conversion changes. Record rejections and open applications separately. |

Compare by role family and source over several weeks. Use actual counts beside rates. For example, 2 recruiter screens out of 8 applications eligible for review is 25% for that small set. Track the age of applications or compare cohorts with similar time to respond. A referral, a recruiter reply and a formal application can describe the same opening. Record both where you discovered it and how you submitted it, then count each stage once per opening.

- **Few eligible leads.** Adjust titles and employer types, and test the France-remote filter plus a separate Paris employer search. Count remote and office requirements as distinct exclusion reasons.
- **Eligible applications with few screens.** Ask a peer or Apec adviser to audit the headline, first half-page and CV evidence against a handful of real ads. Test one revision at a time.
- **Screens with few technical interviews.** Review what you learned about scope, seniority, location and contract. Strengthen a concise example of the work the employer needs.
- **Technical interviews without progression.** Capture what the job tested, practise one repeated scenario and ask for feedback where appropriate.

AI can help organise public ads, suggest alternative titles, and match requirements to your verified experience. If you need a translation, ask it to preserve the distinction between required and preferred skills and flag any ambiguous wording. Have it quote the field it used for each classification and leave missing facts as unknown. Open the canonical listing yourself, check the requisition and work arrangement, and review each message before sending it.

### A four-week starting plan

| Week | Work to finish | What to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Set the two role families and your location and language requirements. Prepare your base CV, save alerts, and screen your first group of postings. | Which titles produced real employer postings with a workable location and working language? |
| 2 | Apply to eligible roles, have two targeted conversations if you can, and ask a peer to check one CV against one real job. | Can someone name your relevant evidence after a quick read? Which questions did recruiters ask? |
| 3 | Rehearse one recurring technical scenario and one project or incident story. If a skill gap is repeated across employers, start a small artifact that addresses it. | Can another person reproduce or interrogate the work? Is it relevant to the roles you actually want? |
| 4 | Compare application cohorts with similar time to respond. Adjust one variable such as titles, employer type, or the CV's first half-page, then measure the next group. | Where is the biggest drop-off? Do you have enough cases to justify a change? |

### Compare an offer with the job you screened

Ask for written confirmation of the legal employer, contract type, title and scope, start date, base salary, variable pay, and work location. Discuss office cadence, exceptions, travel and hours independently. For on-call roles, ask how often engineers are paged, which hours are covered and how standby and interventions are handled. Compare fixed annual gross pay separately from bonus targets and equity. Ask who can answer questions about any compensation you cannot value. Use market ranges as context for discussion. [Pay evidence and limits](market.md#remote-work-hybrid-schedules-and-pay)

> Could you confirm the gross annual base salary, target bonus, contractual work location and expected office schedule? Are the on-call arrangements and any business travel set out separately?
