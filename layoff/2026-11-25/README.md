# IT hiring and AI-related layoffs in the U.S. and EU

Snapshot as of November 25, 2026, covering reporting from November 12 to 25.

AI is already affecting some hiring decisions, especially for junior software roles with a heavy coding workload. Several employers also linked planned workforce reductions to changes in how they invest in or use AI. The evidence for an accelerating AI-driven layoff wave across the U.S. and EU remains limited. The latest official employment figures describe earlier months or quarters.

## U.S. layoff announcements and employment

U.S.-based technology employers announced **171,501 job cuts** from January through October, up **21%** from the same period in 2025. Across all industries, U.S.-based employers cited AI for **121,401 announced cuts**, about **20%** of Challenger’s total and more than double the **54,836** recorded in all of 2025. October had **1,265 AI-cited cuts** and **5,576 announced technology cuts**, the lowest monthly totals of 2026 for both measures. Challenger reports plans and stated reasons. [Challenger October report](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/october-layoffs-at-three-year-low-seasonal-hiring-plans-up-26/) · [Challenger 2025 year-end report](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/2025-year-end-challenger-report-highest-q4-layoffs-since-2008-lowest-ytd-hiring-since-2010/)

The Bureau of Labor Statistics estimated **46,000 layoffs and discharges** in the U.S. information industry in September, up from **38,000** a year earlier. That industry includes software publishing, telecom and media, and the estimate covers all occupations at those employers. CompTIA’s October analysis estimated a **net gain of 26,000 jobs in IT occupations** across the economy, which includes IT workers employed in other industries. [BLS JOLTS, table 5](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_11032026.htm) · [BLS information industry definition](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag51.htm) · [CompTIA October analysis](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tech-employment-gains-momentum-in-october-comptia-analysis-finds-302932161.html)

## EU employment and software job postings

Euro-area information and communication employment fell **1.7% year over year** in Q2. Ireland’s November 16 payroll release showed **123,700** employees in that industry in September, about **400 fewer** than a year earlier. Across the full EU and all industries, Eurostat’s November 13 flash estimate showed employment flat from Q2 to Q3 and **0.4% higher** than a year earlier. Eurofound tracks large, publicly reported restructurings. Its case records and the employment releases leave the EU-wide number of AI-attributed IT layoffs unknown. ## Evidence on AI and hiring

A working paper by MIT and Microsoft Research economists estimated **29% lower quarterly technical hiring after one year** among firms whose scheduled contract reviews prompted them to adopt GitHub Copilot earlier. The decline was concentrated in junior, coding-intensive roles. Employee departures showed no measurable change in that period. [AI-Assisted Programming and Labor Demand, November 6](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-11/AILaborDemand_06Nov2026.pdf)

Coface and the Observatoire des Emplois Menacés et Émergents found that job ads for more AI-exposed occupations, including IT, lagged their sector trends in France. They found similar patterns in Germany and Sweden. The study compares exposure and job postings, so the results leave room for other causes. [Coface AI-LERT study, November 17](https://www.coface.com/fr/actualites-economie-conseils-d-experts/ia-et-emploi-quatre-ans-apres-le-lancement-de-chatgpt-les-offres-d-emploi-des-metiers-les-plus-exposes-decrochent)

## Signs that the weakness is spreading

Several months of rising layoffs, falling payrolls and fewer postings across multiple markets would make the case for a broader downturn stronger. The next scheduled checks are October U.S. JOLTS on **December 1** and Eurostat’s fuller Q3 employment release on **December 7**. Both updates cover periods before the November announcements.
